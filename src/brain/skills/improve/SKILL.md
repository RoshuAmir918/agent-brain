# Improve

The practitioner's pass.
<!-- personal -->
Runs each morning after the night and before the briefing, and asks two questions: how could {agent_name} do one of its existing responsibilities materially better, and did yesterday contain work that should become a responsibility.
<!-- /personal -->
<!-- enterprise -->
Runs each morning after the night, and asks two questions: how could {agent_name} do one of its existing responsibilities materially better, and did yesterday contain work that should become a responsibility.
<!-- /enterprise -->
One investigation a day, carried across days until it is done. Never filler: a morning with nothing to recommend is a one-line ledger row.

<!-- enterprise -->
{organisation}
<!-- /enterprise -->

A responsibility is standing work held under a permission {human_name} gave: `Responsibilities/<id>/RESPONSIBILITY.md` (or an older hub with `skill:`), its method, and records under `$AGENT_HOME/records/`.

Exclude `status: retired` responsibilities from the inventory, investigations and learning candidates. Keep their history; never propose restarting work the owner ended.

## Procedure
<!-- personal -->
Read `brain goals list --json` first: honour each goal's weight (`more` about twice as often, `less` half, `pause` no unasked work) and never repeat work its corrections rule out. Every new investigation or idea names the active goal it advances (`--goal G-n`); with no active goal, propose nothing goal-shaped. Weights don't touch asked work or existing duties.
<!-- /personal -->
<!-- enterprise -->
Read `brain goals list --json` first: {human_name}'s goals, as its leadership states them. Honour each goal's weight (`more` about twice as often, `less` half, `pause` no unasked work) and never repeat work its corrections rule out. Prefer the investigation that moves a goal forward, and name the goal an investigation or idea serves (`--goal G-n`) when it serves one.
<!-- /enterprise -->
1. **Inventory.** Find every responsibility: each `{vault}/Responsibilities/<id>/RESPONSIBILITY.md`, plus any older hub still carrying `skill:` in its frontmatter (`grep -l '^skill:' {vault}/*.md`). Read each hub's Improvements section and the last rows of the improve ledger (`{agent_dir}/Improve/`, or an older vault's `Improvements/`). An improvement in `trialing` whose trial period has passed is assessed first: compare what happened in the records and ledgers with the intended result, then mark it `adopted` or `dropped` with the date and the evidence. Then read `brain ideas --all` for your own open offers and take back any that no longer stand: what it was for ended, it is about how you work, or it is really a decision on existing work. `brain ideas --withdraw <id> --because "<what changed>"`; a decision on existing work then goes where decisions go, the task's `waiting_for_owner` Ask or `brain needs-you --ask`. Withdrawing is not their answer and starts no cooldown. An idea nobody answers lapses on its own after two weeks.
2. **Candidates.** First read `brain ideas --all` and this month's `{agent_dir}/Answered/`: never propose what is open, accepted or declined, or like something {human_name} said to stop asking. Look in four places. *Repeated work:* yesterday's digest in `Conversations/Digests/`, your improve ledger, the week's explore ledger (`{agent_dir}/Explore/`) and open tasks. A request made more than twice, a recurring or endless task (above all a handoff continue moved to daily with no done state, whose work would become the responsibility's living work), or a job done by hand twice is a candidate responsibility. *Finished work:* `brain completed --days 7`. Most need nothing; offer only when its goal is still open or you found something that would uniquely help them that they would not think to ask for. *Goals:* each active goal in `{human_goals}` with evidence in the last sixty days that no responsibility advances; a goal nobody is working toward is the strongest candidate. *New connections and what's possible:* an account connected in the last thirty days (its `{agent_dir}/Channels/History.md` line) that nothing uses and whose connect answer did not rule it out: read what it holds and name the help from that content ("Want a heads-up two days before each contract renewal?"). On Demiurge, read `demiurge guide ideas` against {human_profile} and the goals; propose only what it says works or what you can already do. A few web queries are fine. For a new owner (`brain ideas` says so), lean here. A candidate is standing work, a connection or a new kind of help; one-off preparation is explore's, and how you reason, check or present is step 4's. Offer the strongest as one idea: `brain ideas --add "<proposition>" --why "<what led to it, what they get if yes, what happens if no; at most 60 words>" --needs "<what it takes>" --source <note> --goal G-n`, written like the Ask and Why below. A refusal means not yet. Write the rest into the hub's Improvements as proposals: what the work is, its goal id (`- **Goal:** G-n`), what good looks like, the permission it needs. Propose, never adopt: taking on standing work is {human_name}'s decision, and a goal grants nothing by itself.
3. **Pick one responsibility** to improve, by demonstrated weakness (a lost outcome, a stale strategy, an unused permission), likely benefit, and neglect (the hub whose Improvements were touched longest ago). One that serves an active goal in `{human_goals}` with recent evidence comes before one pursued under an explicit duty; unasked work toward a dormant or set-aside goal earns nothing. An investigation started yesterday and not finished comes first.
4. **Name the gap**, one of four: knowledge or strategy; a broken implementation; a permission {agent_name} holds and does not use; a permission it lacks. The first three are {agent_name}'s to close, never {human_name}'s to approve: they approved the work, not each way of doing it. A better method inside a responsibility {human_name} already approved (how you check a finding, what you show first, what you ask before answering) is written into that responsibility's own skill or records and logged in its hub's Improvements, dated, with `- **Status:** trialing` and how it will be judged; a broken implementation the same way with `adopted`. Neither has an Ask, a Why or an idea. Only the fourth, a permission {agent_name} lacks or new standing work, becomes an idea for {human_name}, with `--needs` naming the permission, and the research still happens so it arrives with a case, not a wish.
5. **Research as a practitioner** would, from real sources, against the actual hub and records.
6. **Write the recommendation** in the hub's Improvements section, for {human_name} to read cold on their phone, since two of its lines are all they see: a `- **Ask:**` line and a `- **Why:**` line on what changes for them, written as Messages to {human_name} below says ("Want me to only reread the mail threads that changed?"). The heading, a plain name for the change, and everything below are your record, at most 150 words: the gap, the one or two facts that show it, the specific move, how it would be judged, one source, and a `- **Status:** proposed` line, dated. Research that does not change the move stays out. A strategy that has not been tested is labelled hypothesis. A proposal is not an improvement, and a change that ran is not a change that worked; the status says which. Each proposal names its active goal with `- **Goal:** G-n`, and its Ask says what it is toward.
7. **Tend what you have learned.** Start from `brain learn account`: a line {human_name} had to repeat was learned and did not reach the work, so first find why (its field's `applies:` missed the work, or its wording did not land) and fix that. Then `brain learn fields`, and `brain learn show "<Field>"` for each field changed since your last run. Merge two lines that say the same lesson: keep the fuller one and strike the other (`brain learn strike "<Field>" <id> --why "merged into ^<id>"`). Promote a candidate that a second, independent piece of evidence now supports (`brain learn promote "<Field>" <id> --evidence "<what showed it again>"`). Strike your own line, never a rule, when newer evidence contradicts it. Keep each field to the dozen lessons that matter most, striking your weakest candidates first. A field is a broad kind of work (Engineering, Writing, Sales), and a handful serve most people: fold a field of one or two lines into the broader one its work belongs to (`brain learn merge "<Field>" "<Broader field>"`, which keeps every line and its id), and give a project its own field only once it holds lessons enough to fill one. Set a field's `applies:` to the words that mark its work when the field missed work it should have reached.
8. **Record.** Write no ledger row: the engine records the run. Its account names the goal id, the responsibility, the gap and the status, or `nothing to recommend`.

## Boundaries
Writes only responsibility hubs' Improvements sections and ideas through `brain ideas --add` and `--withdraw`. Never a skill, a permission, a schedule, the Memory Spec or `{sources_dir}/`, except a repair or a better method in an approved responsibility's own skill (step 4), which changes how it does what it already does, never what it may do; any other skill edit happens in the session where {human_name} adopts, under the recall skill's rule. No sends. No action inside the responsibility itself: its own routine acts, this one studies. A finding never widens what {agent_name} may do; it argues for it.
