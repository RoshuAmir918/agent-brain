# People review

Maintain source-backed profiles and {agent_name}'s own revisable impressions of the people in {human_name}'s life, {human_name} first.

## Procedure
<!-- personal -->
Run `brain portrait coverage --json` for the seed decision only. Read `seeds` in `brain state --json`: an account with a non-null `task` already has an open seed task (its source is `seed:<address>`), including a handed-off onboarding pass. Only for an account in `unreadBackfill` with no open seed task, load `{prefix}seed` and run it scoped to that account before the rest of this pass.
<!-- /personal -->

1. Read the People profiles and histories Sleep 1 wrote, then yesterday's digest in `Conversations/Digests/`. Every user turn labelled with something other than {human_name}'s name is another person speaking: a platform handle or id, or a display name. A handle can be renamed; the stable id on the profile's line for that platform is what identifies them. List the distinct speakers, with what each said and asked and how they responded to the agent.
2. Resolve each speaker to a profile under `{people_dir}/` by the platform id recorded on the profile; for a Demiurge id no profile carries, run `brain people who <id>` and, when listed, create the profile from the template in the canonical folder for the directory name (`{people_dir}/<Name>/Profile.md`, or `{agents_dir}/<Name>/Profile.md` for an intellect), with its id line, never a handle-named stub. A speaker with no matching profile or directory entry is an unknown person: create `{people_dir}/<display name or id>/Profile.md` from the template with the id as its only confirmed identity, the date, and what they said, marked "identity unconfirmed by {human_name}" (the engine treats such a profile as a stranger until {human_name} confirms who it is; then remove the mark), and add an `ask` to CURRENT's `## Needs you`: "a new person messaged me; who is this?" Never merge an unknown id into an existing profile on a name alone.
3. Keep what Sleep 1 already filed from this digest. Do not rewrite the same facts or duplicate a day's History entry. Add only new identity, reception or mailbox evidence and revise the impression when it changes. For each person spoken with whose entry is still missing, write the day's dated entry to `{people_dir}/<Name>/History.md` (newest first: date, channel, what they said and asked, how it went, filed as their claims); a thing coming up for them that they named (a trip, a visit, a result, a date) is written with its date spelled out (`November 2`), so the Upcoming scan carries it into context the week before and update `Profile.md` in place: what they told the agent, filed as their claim with the date; what they asked for and how it was answered (the access log has each request); and the impression. Impressions are the agent's own and say so: how they talk, what they care about, what they expect from the agent, with confidence and counterevidence. Revise an earlier impression when yesterday contradicted it; do not just append. A Profile stays under 300 words and holds only what is stable: identifiers, relationship, impression, what they care about, standing facts, disclosure notes. Dated material belongs in History. A profile that has grown past the budget is split on this pass: move its dated entries to History, keep each fact as one sentence, add the History link at the end of the Profile.
4. Also read the agent's own mailbox for evidence about people. Open specific threads only to verify an identity or a reply; no broad poll, no history backfill. Read each affected profile in full before editing.
5. {human_name}'s own profile, `{human_profile}`, from yesterday's conversations with them. It keeps four sections, under 400 words: **Who** (name, the ids they use on each platform, where they are, timezone), **Working together** (how to address and brief them, hours, channels, what annoys them, and their corrections and directions on your style, format, scope and tone, each dated and merged with its duplicate; under 250 words; it overrides the skills' defaults; how to do a kind of work belongs in a Learned field instead, `brain learn add --field <Field> --claim "<how to act>" --source "<their words, or where you learned it>" [--rule]`), **Their people** (links to groups and key profiles) and **On my own** (what you may do without asking), then a link to their portrait. Each `dismissed stop-asking` row in `{agent_dir}/Answered/` since your last run is {human_name} asking for no questions like it: write it as one dated line in **Working together** ("don't ask about <the kind of thing>, dismissed <date>"), merged with a duplicate; other dismissals (`no`, `done`) close one item and teach nothing beyond it.
   **Goals.** This is the only routine that infers, ends or sets goals aside. Read `brain goals list --json` and `brain goals due --json` before judging them.
   - **Infer goals, nightly.** Read `brain goals refused --json` as well as existing goals. Compare yesterday with earlier days and connected evidence you may read: synced Anki hours, calendar, Canvas, Library works, what {human_name} is building or preparing for. Infer what they repeatedly work toward, never from one passing mention or without evidence. Match refused titles, words and aims; never re-propose one. Merge an existing aim by adding evidence. At most two new inferred goals in a night (including partial runs), and at most five active inferred goals. Write each through `brain goals new "<title>" --words "<their own words quoted from the source>" --said YYYY-MM-DD --inferred --evidence "<date> · <what> · [[note]] or evidence:<source>"`, repeatable evidence. Never paraphrase their words, ask whether it is a goal, or wait for confirmation; silence keeps it. Said and inferred goals both fund preparation now, held, with the same high bar for offers and the same outward permissions. The nightly engine flags an inferred goal stale after thirty days without grounded evidence; edits do not renew it.
   - **Account for goal work, nightly.** Use yesterday's ISO week before noon local, today's week otherwise. Read `brain goals activity --json --weeks 2` and review each goal's items in that target week. When something happened toward a goal since the last people pass, overwrite its account with `brain goals G-n account "<2–4 plain sentences>" --week YYYY-Www`, covering the whole target week so far: what you did, why it served them and what came of it. On Monday morning this finishes the week just ended. Write for {human_name}, with no ids, wikilinks, `.md`, paths, jargon or routine names; say what is held, unused or not sent, and never turn preparation or an offer into a claimed result. Read the items and their details first; never inflate. Skip a goal with no new activity. Only this people routine (`BRAIN_RUN=routine` with the people skill or its cron speaker) or the owner may write accounts. The engine keeps eight ISO weeks and archives older accounts.
   - **Goal endings, nightly.** Judge each active goal against its evidence and recent sources; check `brain goals show G-n` and never end one {human_name} reopened or edited in the last fourteen days (`owner_updated`). At most three endings a night, including endings already dated today: reached → `brain goals G-n reached --evidence "<date> · <what> · [[note]] or evidence:<source>"`; replaced by another active goal → `brain goals G-n superseded-by G-m --evidence "<dated evidence>" [--why "<reason>"]`. Judge every stale-flagged goal in `brain goals due --json`: set it aside with `brain goals G-n set-aside --why "<judged reason>"`, or retain it with a line of new grounded evidence through `brain goals G-n evidence "<date> · <what> · [[note]] or evidence:<source>"`, which clears the stale mark. After each ending, `brain needs-you --confirm "<plain sentence: what you ended and why; you can reopen it>"`. When a goal ends, mark its held Prepared drafts with a dated ending; prime and morning leave out drafts whose `goal:` is not active. A superseded goal's drafts relinked by the engine serve the replacement goal.
   - **Missed dates.** For a due entry whose reasons include a past finish date, `brain needs-you --ask "<goal> was due <date>: move the date, call it reached, or let it go?"`, then `brain goals G-n asked-missed`; a missed date alone never ends it. A stale mark alone is not a missed date.
   - **Quiet said goals.** A said goal (`origin=said`, including a missing origin) with no evidence for sixty days gets one nudge: `brain goals G-n nudge`, then an ask saying "you said <goal> on <date>; nothing since. Still a goal?". `show G-n` carries its `nudged` date. With no evidence or response for thirty more days, `brain goals G-n set-aside --why "No response thirty days after the nudge"`, with the same ending confirm and draft handling. A response keeping it active is evidence and clears the nudge.

6. Each profile's title is the person's name once you know it; their ids stay as platform lines (`- **Slack:** \`U…\``, `- **Demiurge:** \`k57…\``). A profile you cannot resolve says so in its title ("unresolved Demiurge speaker"), never matching a rule by accident. The engine logs requests and raises the declines no rule covers; do not add them to Needs you yourself. Keep `{people_dir}/README.md` a current list of profiles, including any Sleep 1 created for someone only mentioned.
7. Overwrite `$AGENT_HOME/records/people/state.json` with the reviewed window and the ids handled, so a rerun does not count a speaker twice. `brain commit -m "people(<date>)" --push`.

## Boundaries
External services read-only; send nothing. No fixed character labels, no sensitive-trait or mental-health inference, no secrets, no raw message dumps. Preserve {human_name}'s edits.
