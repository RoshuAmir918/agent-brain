# People review

Maintain source-backed profiles and {agent_name}'s own revisable impressions of the people in {human_name}'s life, {human_name} first.

## Procedure
1. Read yesterday's digest in `Conversations/Digests/`. Every user turn labelled with something other than {human_name}'s name is another person speaking: a platform handle or id, or a display name. A handle can be renamed; the stable id on the profile's line for that platform is what identifies them. List the distinct speakers, with what each said and asked and how they responded to the agent.
2. Resolve each speaker to a profile under `{people_dir}/` by the platform id recorded on the profile. A speaker with no matching profile is an unknown person: create `{people_dir}/<display name or id>/Profile.md` from the template with the id as its only confirmed identity, the date, and what they said, marked "identity unconfirmed by {human_name}" (the engine treats such a profile as a stranger until {human_name} confirms who it is; then remove the mark), and add an `ask` to CURRENT's `## Needs you`: "a new person messaged me; who is this?" Never merge an unknown id into an existing profile on a name alone.
3. For each person spoken with, write the day's dated entry to `{people_dir}/<Name>/History.md` (newest first: date, channel, what they said and asked, how it went, filed as their claims); a thing coming up for them that they named (a trip, a visit, a result, a date) is written with its date spelled out (`November 2`), so the Upcoming scan carries it into context the week before and update `Profile.md` in place: what they told the agent, filed as their claim with the date; what they asked for and how it was answered (the access log has each request); and the impression. Impressions are the agent's own and say so: how they talk, what they care about, what they expect from the agent, with confidence and counterevidence. Revise an earlier impression when yesterday contradicted it; do not just append. A Profile stays under 300 words and holds only what is stable: identifiers, relationship, impression, what they care about, standing facts, disclosure notes. Dated material belongs in History. A profile that has grown past the budget is split on this pass: move its dated entries to History, keep each fact as one sentence, add the History link at the end of the Profile.
4. Also read the agent's own mailbox for evidence about people. Open specific threads only to verify an identity or a reply; no broad poll, no history backfill. Read each affected profile in full before editing.
5. {human_name}'s own profile, `{human_profile}`, from yesterday's conversations with them. It keeps six sections, under 500 words: **Who** (name, the ids they use on each platform, where they are, timezone), **Working together** (how to address and brief them, hours, channels, what annoys them, and their corrections to your style, format, scope and tone, each dated and merged with its duplicate; under 150 words; it overrides the skills' defaults), **Their people** (links to groups and key profiles), **What they're about** (work and interests, one line each, linking hubs instead of copying them), **On my own** (what you may do without asking), and **Impression**. The impression is yours and says so. Each point is a pattern you have seen at least twice, stated as behaviour (what they do, decide, ask for, return to, push back on), with the dated conversations it rests on and your confidence. Where what they say about themselves and what they do differ, note both, as behaviour. Nothing about health, mood, relationships or other sensitive traits. Be honest, not flattering. When a point is new or changed, add one `confirm` to CURRENT's `## Needs you` ("I've come to think you … — right?"); count the confirms of this kind in Needs you and History over the last seven days, and add none past two. {human_name}'s answer is evidence: record it beside the point. Revise a point on new evidence, mark it disputed when they disagree and the evidence stands, and never drop one only because they disliked it; a revised point's old version moves to `History.md` beside the profile with the date. Dated material goes to History; your impression of {human_name} is never told to anyone.
6. Each profile's title is the person's name once you know it; their ids stay as platform lines (`- **Slack:** \`U…\``, `- **Demiurge:** \`k57…\``). A profile you cannot resolve says so in its title ("unresolved Demiurge speaker"), never matching a rule by accident. The engine logs requests and raises the declines no rule covers; do not add them to Needs you yourself. Keep `{people_dir}/README.md` current.
7. Overwrite `$HERMES_HOME/records/people/state.json` with the reviewed window and the ids handled, so a rerun does not count a speaker twice. Commit `people(<date>)` and push.

## Boundaries
External services read-only; send nothing. No fixed character labels, no sensitive-trait or mental-health inference, no secrets, no raw message dumps. Preserve {human_name}'s edits. Never load `Dreams/`.
