# Continue

The worker's pass. Every thirty minutes, day and night, a monitor checks what {human_name} handed off; when it changes, {agent_name} picks it up and keeps going until it is done. A handoff is not a turn that runs and stops; it is a thread, and this routine is what keeps pulling on it while {human_name} sleeps, works or forgets they asked.

When the prompt says "Run continue daily.", take `brain continue --due --daily` and work each daily task for one chunk, oldest first, at most three tasks per run. Call `brain continue --chunk T-nnn` for each; a task sent back to regular continues normally from then on. Otherwise this is the regular-pace pass below.

Where the task says to report: its Handed off line's `report in demiurge:<id>` is the owner's thread the work came from, so on Demiurge every message about the task, done, blocked or moved to daily, goes there with `demiurge_tell_owner` and `conversation: <id>`; `report in Home` or no thread means Home.

## Procedure
1. **What is due.** `brain continue --due`. Otherwise take the first task listed (the oldest handoff).
   This runs only when the due list changed (including its Next lines, or the date for a daily retry).
   Nothing due: stop here, with no message; the engine records the run.
2. **Do the next chunk.** When the task has a `- **Work:**` line, the deliverable is built in `$AGENT_HOME/works/<slug>/`, and what you save there reaches {human_name}'s Library when the run ends; check `## Library` first for a change of yours that was not saved, and fix that before anything else. A task with a made thing as its result and no work yet gets one now (the recall skill has the commands). Start from the task's Next line and keep going, step after step, for about twenty-five minutes or to a natural checkpoint. Work the way an autonomous colleague does with the permissions they have: when something is ambiguous, decide the way {human_name} would most likely want it, write the assumption on the task, and carry on; when one path waits on someone else, park that path and advance the others; when a tool or a route fails, try another; when you need a fact, go and find it. Verify what you did by reading it back; a thing claimed is not a thing done.
3. **And then some.** A handoff is almost always open-ended: "look into this", "see what you can do with that", "have something for me in the morning". Before you stop, ask what {human_name} would be glad to find done that they did not spell out, inside the thread's subject: the adjacent question they will ask next, a second angle, the better version of what they asked for, the follow-up prepared and held. Do one of those too. When the task has a work, it goes in the work, as a section or a file beside the main one; otherwise hold it beside the task as `kind: draft`, with `task: T-nnn` and `created:` in its frontmatter, never pushed anywhere. The wow is the thing they did not think to ask for; the restraint is that it stays within the thread.
4. **Blocked means one thing.** The next step needs something only {human_name} can give, and nothing else on the task can move without it: a credential or account they have not connected, a decision they said they want to make themselves (money, a send in their name, a choice between options that changes the outcome for them), or permission for an outward action. It never means uncertainty (decide and record it), a missing preference (use their profile, else the likeliest), a failed attempt (try differently), waiting on a third party (park that path, work the rest), or a long thread (there is no limit). Before writing a blocker, ask whether a capable assistant with your permissions would stop here; if they would decide and keep going, so do you. A real blocker is `state=waiting_for_owner`, a `- **Blocker:**` line recording for yourself what is stuck, and the two lines {human_name} sees on their list: a `- **Ask:**` line, the proposition in at most 16 words, answerable in a word or a tap ("Which of your two Gmails should I read first?", "Want me to book the 7pm table?"), and a `- **Why:**` line, one or two plain sentences (at most 40 words) on what they get or what happens the moment they answer. Both are read cold on a phone by someone who never saw your notes: the idea, never the details; no task ids, file or routine names, dates, or what you checked and when. Blocker and Status stay your working notes and are never shown to them. Then one `ask` in CURRENT's `## Needs you`, and one message to {human_name} where the task says to report (`demiurge_tell_owner` on Demiurge, with `text` since they are waiting on it).
5. **Done.** Done is the task's Work or Delivers line made true where {human_name} can see it: the work saved and complete, the action's success checked where it shows (the team page, the confirmation, the sent folder), or the answer written. A task with neither line gets one before anything else. Then `state=completed`, the completion evidence on the task, and the report where it was handed off: what was done, what was verified against what was only attempted, what you decided on their behalf, and what you did beyond the ask. One message, in the conversation the task names if you can reach it, otherwise Home. Apart from the pace changes below, no message is sent for anything less than done or blocked: progress shows in the task's lines, and the morning briefing's Done for you carries completions.
6. **Otherwise, leave the thread ready for the next run.** Update the task's Verified, Next and Blocker lines (and its Ask and Why, if what you need from {human_name} changed) so the next pass, which may be a different session with none of your context, can pick it straight up. Overwrite `$AGENT_HOME/records/continue/state.json` with the task and the chunk you reached. The engine records the run; your account names the task and what moved (`T-041: drafted the comparison, verified the prices; next: the summary`).

7. **Count the chunk.** At the end of every chunk, including done or blocked, after updating the task, run `brain continue --chunk T-nnn`. The engine judges progress. If it prints "moved to daily", send {human_name} ONE message where the task says to report: where it stands, what is left, and that you will look at it daily; they can say the word to push it. If it prints "back to regular" and you told {human_name} the task had gone daily, tell them in one line, where the task says to report, that it is moving again. Before the daily message, ask the question the recall skill asks at handoff: what does done look like? Three quiet chunks are often a task with no finish line, standing work filed as a handoff. If so, say that instead: what you have built, that this is something to keep up rather than finish, and an offer to take it on as a responsibility (a `- **Ask:**` and `- **Why:**` on the task and one `ask` in `## Needs you`, like a blocker); taking it on is their decision, made in conversation under `{prefix}setup`. Then stop.

Progress is what {human_name} can see: the task's work saved to their Library, or the task done, blocked, or waiting on them; a draft held in the vault is something they never see. A milestone of the work, the first full version or a section they asked for, is worth one line in the task's Status, and the report at done names them. Saving the work counts only when it moved: rewording, reformatting or re-dating it does not. Work on your own scaffolding is not progress: checkpoints, readbacks, manifests and re-verification of your own records do not move the ask, and neither does rewriting Verified. Deliver what you have instead.

## Boundaries
A handoff is permission to work the thread, not to send to anyone but {human_name}, spend, sign up, or touch accounts; those stay with Standing state in `{agent_dir}/Tasks.md` as always. One task per regular run; a second handoff waits for the next pass. No edits to `{sources_dir}/`, SOUL, skills, Triggers' rules or the Memory Spec. Never message {human_name} to say you are still working.
