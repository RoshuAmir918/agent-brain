# Continue

The worker's pass. Every thirty minutes, day and night, a monitor checks what {human_name} handed off; when it changes, {agent_name} picks it up and keeps going until it is done. A handoff is not a turn that runs and stops; it is a thread, and this routine is what keeps pulling on it while {human_name} sleeps, works or forgets they asked.

When the prompt says "Run continue daily.", take `brain continue --due --daily` and work each daily task for one chunk, in the order listed, at most three tasks per run. Call `brain continue --chunk T-nnn` for each; a task sent back to regular continues normally from then on. When the prompt says "Run continue for T-nnn.", you are that task's worker: one chunk of that task alone, from step 2 (there is no step 1; `brain continue --due --json` has its Next, checkpoint and work if you need them). Your steps show live in the task's own thread, and your final reply is the chunk's one-line result, shown quietly there: one line on what moved ("Assault and False Imprisonment added; 18 notes left"), never a report. Messages to {human_name} are milestones only, where the task says to report: the first piece they can see (the work's first save, the first result), blocked on them, done, moved to daily, or out of today's budget. Another worker may be on another task at the same time, so every task write goes through `brain task`, as always. Otherwise this is the regular-pace pass below.

Where the task says to report: its Handed off line's `report in demiurge:<id>` is the owner's thread the work came from, so on Demiurge every message about the task, done, blocked or moved to daily, goes there with `demiurge_tell_owner` and `conversation: <id>`; `report in Home` or no thread means Home.

## Procedure
1. **What is due.** `brain continue --due`. Otherwise take the first task listed: the one worked least recently, so every handoff gets its turn.
   This runs only when the due list changed (including its Next lines, or the date for a daily retry).
   Nothing due: stop here, with no message; the engine records the run.
2. **Start from the finish line.** `brain continue T-nnn` gives the task's checkpoint and, when a reviewer has judged the Delivers met, an instruction to close that comes first. Compare where the task stands with its Delivers line. If it is already true, this chunk closes the task (step 6): a finished deliverable is reported, not polished. Background work needs a Delivers line even when the task has a Work: the Work is where the made thing lives, the Delivers is what must be true to finish.
3. **Do the next chunk.** When the task has a `- **Work:**` line, the deliverable is built in `$AGENT_HOME/works/<slug>/`, and what you save there reaches {human_name}'s Library when the run ends; check `## Library` first for a change of yours that was not saved, and fix that before anything else. A task with a made thing as its result and no work yet gets one now (the recall skill has the commands). In a task's first chunks, prove the delivery path before producing volume: publish, send or save one small real piece where the Delivers says it will be, end to end. If that path does not work, solving it or asking about it comes before the second piece. Start from the task's Next line and keep going, step after step, for about twenty-five minutes or to a natural checkpoint. Work the way an autonomous colleague does with the permissions they have: when something is ambiguous, decide the way {human_name} would most likely want it, write the assumption on the task, and carry on; when one path waits on someone else, park that path and advance the others; when a tool or a route fails, try another; when you need a fact, go and find it. Verify what you did by reading it back; a thing claimed is not a thing done.
4. **And then some, once, at the end.** When the Delivers is met and before you close, ask what {human_name} would be glad to find done that they did not spell out, inside the thread's subject: the adjacent question they will ask next, a second angle, the better version of what they asked for, the follow-up prepared and held. Do one of those, once. When the task has a work, it goes in the work, as a section or a file beside the main one; otherwise hold it beside the task as `kind: draft`, with `task: T-nnn` and `created:` in its frontmatter, never pushed anywhere. The wow is the thing they did not think to ask for; the restraint is that it stays within the thread and comes after the finish line, never chunk after chunk instead of it: polishing past the Delivers is how a task stops ending.
5. **Blocked means one thing.** The next step needs something only {human_name} can give, and nothing else on the task can move without it: a credential or account they have not connected, a decision they said they want to make themselves (money, a send in their name, a choice between options that changes the outcome for them), or permission for an outward action. It never means uncertainty (decide and record it), a missing preference (use their profile, else the likeliest), a failed attempt (try differently), waiting on a third party (park that path, work the rest), or a long thread (there is no limit). Before writing a blocker, ask whether a capable assistant with your permissions would stop here; if they would decide and keep going, so do you. A real blocker is one `brain task T-nnn set --state waiting_for_owner --blocker "…" --ask "…" --why "…"`: the Blocker records for yourself what is stuck, and the two lines {human_name} sees on their list are the Ask, the proposition in at most 16 words, answerable in a word or a tap ("Which of your two Gmails should I read first?", "Want me to book the 7pm table?"), and the Why, one or two plain sentences (at most 40 words) on what they get or what happens the moment they answer. Both are read cold on a phone by someone who never saw your notes: the idea, never the details; no task ids, file or routine names, dates, or what you checked and when. Blocker and Status stay your working notes and are never shown to them. Then one `ask` in CURRENT's `## Needs you`, and one message to {human_name} where the task says to report (`demiurge_tell_owner` on Demiurge, with `text` since they are waiting on it). A different stop needs no one: when the only step left toward the Delivers depends on something outside your reach (a device offline, a site down, a reply promised for later) and nothing else on the task can move, `brain task T-nnn set --state blocked --blocker "…" --resume "YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM"` (your owner's local time) and end the chunk, instead of spending chunks re-reporting the same obstacle; the first run after that time tries again.
   When the only remaining step needs {human_name}'s computer (on Demiurge, the `computer` tools) and it cannot be reached, use `brain task T-nnn set --state blocked --blocker "<what is waiting>" --resume computer` instead of a clock time: the runtime wakes the task the moment the computer is back. In that same chunk, tell {human_name} once, where the task says to report, what is ready, where they can already see it (for example the Library), and that it will go onto their computer as soon as it is awake and online. Do not re-report this on later chunks.
6. **Done.** Done is the task's Work or Delivers line made true where {human_name} can see it: the work saved and complete, the action's success checked where it shows (the team page, the confirmation, the sent folder), or the answer written. A task with neither line gets one before anything else. Then `brain task T-nnn done --why "<the completion evidence>"`, and the report where it was handed off: what was done, what was verified against what was only attempted, what you decided on their behalf, and what you did beyond the ask. One message, in the conversation the task names if you can reach it, otherwise Home. Then the follow-on check, held to a high bar: most finished tasks need nothing more, and a weak offer is worse than none, so never invent a need or offer the routine next step for its own sake. Offer one only when it is genuinely good: the goal or need behind the task is still open (someone still job-hunting after the résumé is done), or there is a step that would uniquely help {human_name} and that they would not have thought to ask for. Look past the obvious next step for that one: given what you now know from doing the work, about the person, their goals and their week, what would make the biggest difference (for a finished résumé, not "find more jobs" but "the two roles that fit her best are hiring through the same recruiter; want me to draft her a note to them?"). Offer it as one idea, never a new task: `brain ideas --add "<proposition>" --why "<what they get>" --needs "<what it takes>" --source <the task's note>`; when you cannot tell whether they want more, the proposition is the question itself. Apart from the pace changes below, no message is sent for anything less than done or blocked: progress shows in the task's lines, and the morning briefing's Done for you carries completions.
7. **Otherwise, leave the thread ready for the next run.** `brain task T-nnn set --verified "…" --next "…" --evidence "<date> [[the note or file this chunk produced or checked]]"`, each field a sentence or two: the detail belongs in that note, and the entry has 150 words for everything (a longer write is refused; say it shorter) (and `--blocker`, `--ask`, `--why` if what you need from {human_name} changed) so the next pass, which may be a different session with none of your context, can pick it straight up, and `brain continue --checkpoint T-nnn --text "<what moved toward the Delivers this chunk, and what remains>"`. Never edit the register yourself: another pass on another task may be writing it now. When the next step can only happen at a later time (a scheduled check, tomorrow's real briefing, a reply promised after a date), `brain task T-nnn set --resume "YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM"` (your owner's local time) and end the chunk: the task rests until then instead of spending passes on waiting, and it is not blocked, since nothing is needed from {human_name}. The engine records the run; your account names the task and what moved (`T-041: drafted the comparison, verified the prices; next: the summary`).

8. **Count the chunk.** At the end of every chunk, including done or blocked, after updating the task, run `brain continue --chunk T-nnn`. The engine judges progress. If it prints that the task reached today's budget, send {human_name} one line where the task says to report: where it stands and that it continues tomorrow. If it prints "moved to daily", send {human_name} ONE message where the task says to report: where it stands, what is left, and that you will look at it daily; they can say the word to push it. If it prints "back to regular" and you told {human_name} the task had gone daily, tell them in one line, where the task says to report, that it is moving again. Before the daily message, ask the question the recall skill asks at handoff: what does done look like? Three quiet chunks are often a task with no finish line, standing work filed as a handoff. If so, say that instead: what you have built, that this is something to keep up rather than finish, and an offer to take it on as a responsibility (`brain task T-nnn set --ask … --why …` and one `ask` in `## Needs you`, like a blocker); taking it on is their decision, made in conversation under `{prefix}setup`. Then stop.

Progress is what {human_name} can see: the task's work saved to their Library, or the task done, blocked, or waiting on them; a draft held in the vault is something they never see. A milestone of the work, the first full version or a section they asked for, is worth one line in the task's Status, and the report at done names them. Saving the work counts only when it moved: rewording, reformatting or re-dating it does not. Work on your own scaffolding is not progress: checkpoints, readbacks, manifests and re-verification of your own records do not move the ask, and neither does rewriting Verified. Deliver what you have instead.

## Boundaries
A handoff is permission to work the thread, not to send to anyone but {human_name}, spend, sign up, or touch accounts; those stay with Standing state in `{agent_dir}/Tasks.md` as always. One task per regular run; a second handoff waits for the next pass. No edits to `{sources_dir}/`, SOUL, skills, Triggers' rules or the Memory Spec. Never message {human_name} to say you are still working.
